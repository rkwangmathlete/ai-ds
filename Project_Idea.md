This project investigates why some marathon runners experience a significant late-race slowdown, or "hitting the wall," while others maintain a steady pace to the finish. Using individual results from the 2015, 2016, and 2017 Boston Marathons alongside weather data, I will engineer features such as segment pace, pace variability, pace decay rate, and age-graded performance, then train and compare machine learning models to classify which runners are likely to hit the wall based only on their early-race data. This matters because pacing strategy is one of the most controllable factors in endurance performance, and understanding which combination of age, early pace, and weather conditions best predicts a late race collapse could help runners and coaches design smarter, more personalized race strategies. The dataset consists of individual-level race results (age, gender, location, and checkpoint split times) for tens of thousands of runners across three years, supplemented with historical race-day weather records for each year.
